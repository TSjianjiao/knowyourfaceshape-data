# How to Contour a Round Face: Finding the Line on Your Own Face, Not on a Diagram

Live page: <https://knowyourfaceshape.com/blog/how-to-contour-a-round-face/>

## Eight years of placement advice, side by side

*Round-face contour placement as published, oldest first — the columns that disagree are the ones that moved*

| Published | Where it appeared | Shade depth | The cheek line | Jaw, forehead and chin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| September 2018 | 100 Percent Pure — a skincare and cosmetics brand's how-to blog | 1–2 deeper | Just under the cheekbone, located by landmark: where the sideburn and the ear tragus sit. | Contour the outer forehead at the hairline and the temples, and the far edges of the jawline. Highlight the centre of the forehead and the centre of the chin. |
| April 2020 | Makeup & Hair by Kendra — an independent artist's placement guide | none given | Start at the hairline by the ear and aim down toward the lip line, stopping where a vertical dropped from the outer corner of the iris would fall. | Stay away from the top of the forehead and away from the chin. Aim the line at the chin rather than the lip line for more length. Highlight must not pass the eyebrows. |
| February 2022 | MasterClass — an online-education platform's beauty how-to article | 2–3 deeper (1 if very fair) | Under the cheekbone, drawn as one continuous “3” from the temple down the side of the face and curving out under the jawline; no landmark is given for finding the bone itself. | Contour the temples and along the jawline; avoid the chin and the very top of the forehead, which it says darkening would make the face read rounder. Highlight the centre of the forehead, the centre of the chin and an upside-down triangle under the eyes. |
| May 2025 | Charlotte Tilbury — a luxury cosmetics house | none given | Along the cheekbone in an angular motion, from the top of the ear toward the apples of the cheeks. | Then from the outside of the jaw toward the chin. The forehead is not mentioned for this shape at all. |
| December 2025 | Smytten — a beauty-commerce how-to guide | 1–2 deeper, cool | Two fingers laid horizontally under the eye; the contour starts where the fingers end — above the hollow, not in it. | Contour the underside of the jawbone and blend down onto the neck. The temples get a C-shaped stroke blended upward and outward. A narrow strip of highlight runs down the centre of the forehead. |
| April 2026 | Test My Face — a face-analysis site | none given | Start at the widest part of the cheekbone and sweep along the outer contour toward the jaw, blending inward rather than down. | Blush swept vertically downward instead of across. Matte highlight to the mid-face, the T-zone and the chin. The forehead is not part of the round-face contour. |

> Every date in the first column was read off the rendered page, not out of a hidden meta tag or schema field — but the column is not one kind of date, and which row is which should be stated. Five of the six print a full day, and those days — 12 April 2020, 10 February 2022, 30 May 2025, 8 December 2025 and 7 April 2026 — are shortened to month and year here only to keep the column even with the one that prints no day at all. Two of the six are read from something other than a publication line, and they are the two to be careful with. The September 2018 row prints no day anywhere: that month and year appears once, in the footer tag list, which makes the oldest end of the span a footer tag rather than a dateline. The February 2022 row is a “Last updated 10 February 2022” line — a revision stamp, so it dates the most recent change to the MasterClass page, not when that page was first published; it may have gone up earlier and been revised into the date shown. The other four print their date in full on the page. None of this changes any placement in the table, but it does mean the eight-year span is softer at the 2018 end, and that the 2022 row is a latest-change date sitting where a publication date would otherwise be — so do not read the exact order around those two as firmly as the middle four. “None given” means the page describes placement without committing to a shade depth. The names are here so that each row can be checked by somebody who is not us. No links are given to any of them.
