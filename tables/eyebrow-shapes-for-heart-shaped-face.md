# Eyebrows for a Heart-Shaped Face: Peak Position, Brow Height and the Forehead

## Where the peak goes: four sources, one place, none of them citing another

*Four sources naming the peak's position, in the order they were published, and the distance between what they say*

| Source, by date and kind | Where it puts the peak | As a fraction of brow length | What it was addressing |
|---|---|---|---|
| February 2016 — a review of ideal brow shape and position | On a vertical line directly above the lateral limbus | not stated; the position is a landmark, not a fraction | The ideal brow in general. Also warns that a peak placed too far inward reads as surprised, and a low medial brow with a high lateral peak reads as angry. |
| July 2017 — an observational case series, 60 women, each positioning her own ideal brow with her fingers across five photographs | Actually found: between the lateral limbus and the lateral canthus in 58.3% (35 of 60); at the lateral canthus 28.3% (17); at the lateral limbus 10% (6); no detectable peak 3.3% (2). Wanted: lateral canthus 33.3%, between canthus and limbus 66.6% | not stated; face shape was never recorded | Where the peak is and where the same women wanted it. The two desired categories total 99.9%, leaving 0.1% for anything else; the textbook's limbus is where 10 of the 60 actually had their peak. |
| July 2026 — a clinical review's face-shape table, itself reproduced from another publication | In between the lateral limbus and the lateral canthus line | not stated | This face shape by name. The only entry in the record that prescribes a peak position to this outline, in a table carrying no measurement. |
| October 2024 — two replies to one asker, both written on the same day | About two-thirds toward the temple, and not halfway; past the pupil, and not dead centre with it | 2/3, stated as a fraction, and stated against the halfway position it is rejecting | An asker choosing between photographed options. No face shape was mentioned by either side, and neither had read either paper. |

> The middle third of a brow runs from its inner start to one-third of its length; the lateral third from two-thirds to the end. The junction between them is therefore at two-thirds of the total, which is why a 2026 clinical statement about gender and a 2024 answer about temples name the same place. The 58.3%, 28.3%, 10% and 3.3% are 35, 17, 6 and 2 of 60; 33.3% and 66.6% are the same study's desired-position figures and sum to 99.9%. Landmarks: the lateral canthus is the outer corner of the eye, the lateral limbus is the outer edge of the iris. Sources are described here by kind and date and are never linked.

## Brow height against the forehead sitting under it

*What removing hair from the top edge costs, at three starting brow heights, against a 50 mm forehead*

| Starting brow height | Cut 1 mm from the top | Cut 2 mm | Cut 3 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.00 mm — 0.16 in (the bottom of the measured range) | 3.00 mm / 0.12 in left — 25.0% of the brow gone | 2.00 mm / 0.08 in — 50.0% gone | 1.00 mm / 0.04 in — 75.0% gone |
| 7.51 mm — 0.30 in (the measured mean) | 6.51 mm / 0.26 in — 13.3% gone | 5.51 mm / 0.22 in — 26.6% gone | 4.51 mm / 0.18 in — 39.9% gone |
| 13.00 mm — 0.51 in (the top of the measured range) | 12.00 mm / 0.47 in — 7.7% gone | 11.00 mm / 0.43 in — 15.4% gone | 10.00 mm / 0.39 in — 23.1% gone |

> The three starting values are the floor, the mean and the ceiling of a measured range across 60 women (mean 7.51 mm, standard deviation 1.85). Brow height is the vertical distance from the top of the brow to the horizontal line through the inferior boundary of its medial portion. The forehead side of the trade is the same in every row: a 1 mm cut adds 1 mm to a 50 mm forehead, which is 2.0% and 0.04 in; 2 mm adds 4.0% and 0.08 in; 3 mm adds 6.0% and 0.12 in. That makes the exchange rate 12.5 to 1 against on a 4 mm brow, 6.7 to 1 on the mean, and 3.8 to 1 on a 13 mm brow. Inch figures are the millimetre figure divided by 25.4, exact since 1959, rounded to two decimals.
