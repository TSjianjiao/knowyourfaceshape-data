# Eyebrow Shapes for an Oblong or Long Face: Tail, Arch and Brow Height

Live page: <https://knowyourfaceshape.com/blog/eyebrow-shapes-for-oblong-faces/>

## Making level and a little into numbers you can check

*Tail drop for a 25 mm tail run at four tilts, so that level stops being a mood and becomes something you can measure against*

| Tilt of the tail below level | Drop in mm | Drop in inches | What it reads as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° — level | 0.00 mm | 0.00 in | the tip finishes at the height of the inner start |
| 5° | 2.19 mm | 0.09 in | looks level, is not; the whole visible difference is under a tenth of an inch |
| 20° | 9.10 mm | 0.36 in | visibly down, and the point where the lengthening claim starts to bite |
| 30° | 14.43 mm | 0.57 in | what the record's one angle describes, if that corner was measured from the horizontal |

> Drop = tail run × tan(tilt). The run is the horizontal distance from the highest point of the brow to the tip of the tail; 25 mm is a stand-in input, not a target, so substitute your own. Every inch figure is the millimetre figure divided by 25.4, exact since 1959, rounded to two decimals. At a 20 mm run the same four tilts give 0.00, 1.75, 7.28 and 11.55 mm — so the run matters more than the angle, which is why nobody in the record who talked about the tail ever asked how long it was.

## Nearly five years of advice on this face, in the order it was given

*Every position in the record, oldest first. Coverage runs 8 October 2021 to 31 July 2026 — four years, nine months and twenty-three days.*

| Date | Who it was said to | What it said about the brow | What the later record did with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 Oct 2021 | A person describing their own oblong face and sparse brows | An oblong face with sparse brows. One answer, given by someone who says they could not see the face, states the shape rule: straight brows, or straight with a slight upward slant, because a brow that goes down at the end makes the face appear longer. Others say to set the hairs upright and draw small individual hairs at the inner start so the two ends sit closer together, and to have pigment implanted with a slight arch. | Repeated and never tested. It is the only instruction in the record that ties a brow direction to this face shape by name, it was given without sight of the face, and nobody in the following four years and nine months supplies a reason for it beyond that one sentence. |
| 1 Jul 2022 | A person naming a long face and inherited heavy brows, asking for makeup or brow advice | A long face and inherited heavy brows. Contouring and curtain bangs are what come back first, and neither is a brow. Of the brow answers: a subtle arch will lift the face more; brush up and out to define an arch; shorten the brows because the tails make the eyes look droopy; the brows sit low toward the nose, so pluck a few hairs underneath and raise them a little; make eyes, lips and brows take up more visual space so the face reads as more even. One answer gives the only mechanism in the record — a straight-across fringe plus strong brows makes one continuous horizontal line, and the line is what reads as length. One gives the only angle: the brows form a sideways L and the corner should open from a right angle to about 120 degrees. | Split four ways and never resolved. The arch instruction, the shorten-the-tail instruction and the raise-the-front instruction all appear here for the first time in the record, and all three are still being given unchanged in 2026. The mechanism was one answer among hundreds; the angle appeared once and nowhere else. |
| 29 Jan 2023 | A person describing over-plucked brows, an oblong face, and filler placed to make the face look less long | Originally thick brows over-plucked in order to lift them, an oblong face, and filler placed to make the face look less long. One answer says the brows read as a dated thin arch and to practise, with eyebrow templates to help at first. Another says to look into a growth serum but avoid any containing prostaglandin, because it causes fat loss around the eyes. A third recommends a specific over-the-counter serum, reports it filled a bare patch left by a piercing, and follows it with a conditioning oil at night. | The only reported outcome in the record, and it is one sentence with nothing measured: the filler did not seem to work well, and the next sentence is a plan for jaw surgery. The prostaglandin caution names a real documented effect of a class of eye drops, and no source in the record — including this page — distinguishes a drop from a serum. |
| 3 Jun 2023 | A person asking whether brows they already like are too high for a long mid-face | The question is whether these brows are too high, because higher brows are liked but seem to make the middle of the face look longer. One answer says the height is right as it is and that the middle is what should fill in. Another asks what the middle third is meant to cover — the nose area? — and says the upper face is the strongest thing there. | Unanswered, and it is the only place in the record where the raise-the-front instruction is examined from the receiving end. That instruction was given in 2022 and given again in 2026, two years and eight months after this; neither refers to it. The counter-question about what the middle third means is also the only recognition anywhere in the record that long is not one measurement. |
| 13 Jan 2025 | A person describing brows that look too long and downturned at the tail | The brows look too long and downturned at the tail. The answers divide: too long at the inner end over the nose; the length is right, with the inner edge at the nostril and the outer edge at the outer corner of the eye; do not arch from the top and bottom so much; and the longest argument in the record — the brows do not need to be shorter at all, because horizontally short brows are a byproduct of repeated professional reshaping, the tail hairs being the sparsest and the first to be tweezed whenever a shape is changed in the chair. Someone describing themselves as over fifty says to leave them alone, because waxing or plucking long enough can stop the hair returning. The five-week cleanup interval belongs to the person asking, not to the advice. | This is where the tail argument changes subject. No face shape is mentioned anywhere in that exchange. The claim is about what a reference photograph means, and it dissolves the 2022 instruction rather than contradicting it: the two sides are comparing the person asking to different images. It is also the only source in the record that supplies a time horizon and the only one that supplies a maintenance interval. |
| 21 Feb 2026 | A person listing an oblong face, a large forehead, small downturned hooded eyes and thick low brows, wanting practical advice rather than reassurance | An oblong face, a large forehead, small downturned hooded eyes and thick low brows, with practical advice asked for and reassurance explicitly not wanted. One answer gives a numbered routine in which the brow step is to brush and groom them and thin them a little from the underside so they do not sit as low; the eye step beside it is a short wing taken straight out rather than upturned, on the outer quarter of the lid only. Another says to have the brows shaped professionally. Another says to look up tutorials aimed at the specific feature. | The raise-the-front instruction restated three years, seven months and twenty days after it was first given, in nearly the same words, still with no number, and sitting on the far side of a report that raising the front made a face look longer. The hooded lids named in the question are answered with eyeliner rather than with brow placement — the second time in the record that this co-occurring feature is skipped. |
| 31 Jul 2026 | A person asking about the face as a whole rather than about the brow | A question that drew one answer. That answer says the face looks oblong or oval, that the eyes are round with a positive tilt, and that the end of the eyebrows should be extended — alongside reducing body fat. | The only place in the record that says to lengthen the tail, and the last word chronologically, so the oldest and newest instructions in the record point in opposite directions. It is also the only answer that could not choose between oblong and oval, and it then gave both the same answer — which section eight shows is defensible inside the band the two shapes share and undecided outside it. |

> Five phrasings a person with this problem would actually type were searched, in twelve searches: oblong face and eyebrows; an eyebrow shape for a long face; the best eyebrows for an oblong face; eyebrows for a long thin face; and eyebrows making the face look longer. Seven sources qualified. To qualify, a source had to be a real person describing this face shape or a real person answering one, had to carry a retrievable date, and had to contain an instruction about the brow rather than only reassurance. Six hundred and thirty separate pieces of advice were read across the seven. Excluded, and the exclusion is the interesting part: the three largest bodies of brow advice turned up by the same searches contain no long-face material at all and are not in this table, so what follows is not the loudest record available, only the on-topic one. Also excluded: material whose only shape talk was surgical; one source on an oval-or-oblong face with full cheeks whose brow advice is already used on this site's oval-face eyebrow page; and anything with no retrievable date. Sources are described here by who was asked and what was said, and are never linked.
