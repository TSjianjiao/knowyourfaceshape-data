## The first table: what width at the sides is worth

*Outline ratio after adding width at the sides, at the 136 mm working width — millimetres in, read out as a ratio*

| Your face-length ratio | Face length at 136 mm (5.35 in) | No width added | +5 mm (0.20 in) each side | +10 mm (0.39 in) each side | +15 mm (0.59 in) each side | +20 mm (0.79 in) each side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.47 | 199.9 mm / 7.87 in | 1.47 | 1.37 | 1.28 | 1.20 | 1.14 |
| 1.65 | 224.4 mm / 8.83 in | 1.65 | 1.54 | 1.44 | 1.35 | 1.27 |
| 1.83 | 248.9 mm / 9.80 in | 1.83 | 1.70 | 1.60 | 1.50 | 1.41 |

> Each cell is face length ÷ (cheekbone width + twice the width added), carried out at the 136 mm cheekbone line this site uses on its eyewear pages and rounded to two decimals, so the arithmetic can be redone on a phone: at 1.65 the length is 1.65 × 136 = 224.4 mm, and 224.4 ÷ (136 + 20) = 1.44. Inches in the headers are the millimetres divided by 25.4. The three rows are not invented: 1.65 is the centre this site's rule set puts on this family and 1.47 and 1.83 are one published standard deviation either side of it (0.18 on this scale). The four stops are chosen rather than measured and are marked as such — nobody's perm is specified in millimetres, and nothing stands behind the five-millimetre spacing except that it is the finest division a hand can judge in a mirror and a tape reads without squinting. The rate falls as you buy more: on the 1.65 row the first 5 mm of the 10 mm each side takes 0.113 off the ratio and the second takes 0.099, so the price paid in each column is slightly less than the column before it. Twenty millimetres per side comes to 0.375 of the ratio, which is 2.06 of those published standard deviations — a long face wearing that much width does not read as a slightly shorter long face, it reads as another shape altogether, which is why the two columns in the middle of the table are the working ones and the last is a ceiling.

## The exchange rate between the two directions

*What one millimetre of each lever is worth at the 136 mm line, and what the same job costs in the other currency*

| Lever | Ratio moved per millimetre | In published deviations (0.18) | The same job in the other currency | Does the rate depend on your ratio? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Width added at each side, at cheekbone height | 0.0216 at 1.47 · 0.0243 at 1.65 · 0.0269 at 1.83 | 0.119 · 0.133 · 0.148 | 3.3 mm / 0.13 in hidden at the hairline does the same as 1 mm per side at 1.65 | Yes — it rises with it |
| Forehead covered by a fringe | 0.00735 at every ratio | 0.040 | 0.30 mm / 0.012 in per side does the same as 1 mm of fringe | No — it is one over your width |
| Height stacked at the crown | 0.00735, added rather than removed | 0.040, against you | 5 mm / 0.20 in of height is repaid by 1.5 mm per side at 1.65 | No |
| A beard line or shading dropped below the chin | 0.00735, added | 0.040, against you | Same repayment: 1.5 mm per side for 5 mm below the chin | No |

> The width rate is twice the face length over the square of the cheekbone width, evaluated at zero added width; the length rate is one over the cheekbone width. Both are the first millimetre's price — the width rate is about 40% lower by the time twenty millimetres per side have been added, which is why the fourth section is built by solving the exact fraction rather than by multiplying this rate. The equality in the last two columns is the neat part of the whole page: the number of millimetres at the hairline needed to equal one millimetre per side is exactly twice your ratio, 2.94 at the bottom of the band, 3.30 at its centre and 3.66 at the long end. The repayment figures quoted for the crown and the chin are that quotient run backwards, at 1.65.

## How much width to stop reading as long

*Millimetres needed at each side of the face to bring the outline read down to three published landmarks*

| 1.65 | 128.2 mm / 5.05 in | 0.8 mm / 0.03 in | 7.8 mm / 0.31 in | 8.8 mm / 0.35 in |
| 1.65 | 136 mm / 5.35 in | 0.8 mm / 0.03 in | 8.3 mm / 0.33 in | 9.4 mm / 0.37 in |
| 1.65 | 148.8 mm / 5.86 in | 0.9 mm / 0.04 in | 9.1 mm / 0.36 in | 10.3 mm / 0.40 in |
| 1.83 | 128.2 mm / 5.05 in | 7.9 mm / 0.31 in | 15.7 mm / 0.62 in | 16.8 mm / 0.66 in |
| 1.83 | 136 mm / 5.35 in | 8.3 mm / 0.33 in | 16.7 mm / 0.66 in | 17.8 mm / 0.70 in |
| 1.83 | 148.8 mm / 5.86 in | 9.1 mm / 0.36 in | 18.2 mm / 0.72 in | 19.5 mm / 0.77 in |

> Each cell is cheekbone width × (your ratio ÷ target − 1) ÷ 2, so it is a division and a multiplication and can be redone for a face outside the published adult band — 128.2 and 148.8 mm are the ends of that band, one standard deviation either side of the measured sex means this site published, and 136 mm is its working line. Cross-check with the first table rather than trusting this one: the 1.65 row at 136 mm says 8.3 mm per side reaches 1.47, and the first table's 1.65 row shows +10 mm per side landing at 1.44, which is on the right side of the same border. The equivalence worth carrying out of the shop: 8.3 mm per side is the same move as 27.5 mm (1.08 in) of forehead under a fringe, because the quotient is twice the ratio. At the long end the same border costs 16.7 mm per side, or 61.0 mm (2.40 in) of forehead — more forehead than most faces have above the brows, which is the arithmetic reason the long rectangle wears length in waves rather than in a fringe alone.
