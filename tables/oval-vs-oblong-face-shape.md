| This site's detector, raw landmark scale (12 September 2026) | — | 1.20 is an ordinary reading; 1.40 is above anything in the set |

> The last row is not a border but a scale, and it is the reason the other four disagree with your tape. Across the 50 photographs in the calibration set the detector's own length ratio spans 1.075 to 1.305, because its landmarks sit below the hairline and outside the cheekbone arch. It is not the number a tape gives, which is why every figure this site shows you is converted to the tape scale before it is printed.

## The four borders, in the order they were published

*Eleven published positions on the oval – oblong border, sorted by publication date. Rows 1 to 5 and row 7 offered no number at all; rows 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11 did, and they do not agree.*

| Date | Who published it | The border it draws |
|---|---|---|
| 2013-06-30 | A practising cosmetologist, answering a reader who had posted a photograph | No ratio offered. The criterion is the side line: temple to jaw entirely vertical means oblong, described as “a longer oval”. Adds that the rules run slightly differently for men than for women. |
| 2013-06-30 | A cosmetology student, citing the textbook their school publishes | No ratio exists to give: “there is no measurement guideline for face shape because everyone is different.” The book is only sold to students of the school that publishes it, so a reader cannot check it. |
| 2019-03-24 | Three readers, one posted photograph, one day | Oblong with a touch of diamond; oval; heart, because the widest point sits at the temple. Three labels, and none of the three took a measurement. |
| 2020-07-26 | A reader answering another reader who had used a tape | “Oblong is longer and more narrow than oval.” Asked whether 6 – 7 in by 5 in is oval: “possibly, but it's hard to tell just by measurements.” |
| 2024-09-26 | Ten readers, one posted photograph | Six oval, three heart, one round-oval mix. The person asking had already been given different answers by apps, by websites and by an AI tool. |
| 2025-11-22 | An editorial guide on a face-shape tool's site, author named | Oblong starts at 1.6:1, oval sits near 1.5:1, length divided by cheekbone width. Also puts the oblong jaw angle at 105 – 115°. |
| 2026-04-19 | A reader writing at length about facial aesthetics | No ratio. Oval means cheekbones wider than jaw, so the mid and lower face taper; widen the jaw on the same outline and you have a square. |
| 2026-07-16 | A guide site devoted to a single shape | Oblong starts at 1.5, oval runs 1.25 – 1.45, and 1.45 – 1.5 is a borderline band it calls the long oval — length divided by the average of three widths. The same page publishes a prevalence split (28.4% oval, 21.7% oblong, from “51,247+ analyses”) and “92%+ accuracy”, both self-reported with no stated method, and ties the oblong outline to a 1.618 proportion. None of the three is checkable, and none of it is repeated here as evidence. |
| 2026-08-14 | An AI detector's comparison article | Oblong starts near 1.75:1 or more, oval near 1.5:1, divided by cheekbone width — and it states plainly that there is no hard cutoff between a 1.5:1 and a 1.75:1 face. |
| 2026-09-11 | This site's comparison page, as first published | Oblong starts at 1.65, oval runs 1.45 – 1.5, with 1.55 named as the grey zone. Superseded by the version you are reading. |
| 2026-09-12 | This site's published rule set, after recalibration | Oblong centred at 1.65 ± 0.18, oval at 1.45 ± 0.18, on the tape scale. The two bands share 1.47 to 1.63, which is why this site reports a second shape instead of forcing one. |

> Identities are given by type, not by name or link, for every row that is not this site. Dates are the publication date printed on the page or, where a page carries none, the date the position was written down.
